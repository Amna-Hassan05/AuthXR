# VR Controller Interaction Analysis

**Source:** `VRInteractionLogger`  
**Log File:** `odh_logs_2026-10-18_17_46_58_772.txt`  
**Analysis Date:** October 19, 2026

## 1. Overview

This project analyses controller movement data collected from a Unity-based VR application running on a headset.

The original log contained approximately 22,000 lines. Entries generated by `VRInteractionLogger` were extracted and converted into CSV format to create a structured time series of Left and Right controller poses.

The analysis focuses on controller movement patterns, hand dominance, movement speed, synchronized controller motion, and potential locomotion events during the VR session.

### Data Fields

Each record contains the following information:

- `timestamp`: Timestamp associated with the logged event.
- `app_time`: Application-relative time in seconds.
- `event_type`: Type of logged event.
- `controller_name`: Identifies the Left or Right controller.
- `position_x`, `position_y`, `position_z`: Controller position coordinates.
- `rotation_x`, `rotation_y`, `rotation_z`, `rotation_w`: Quaternion components representing controller orientation.
- `speed`: Recorded controller speed.

### Derived Features

The following additional columns were used during analysis:

- `hand`: Identifies the Left or Right hand.
- `device`: Identifies the device as a controller.
- `is_moving`: Binary indicator set to `1` when speed exceeds `0.01`, otherwise `0`.

**Data continuity:** The movement data is generally continuous, with one gap of approximately 0.7 seconds near 11.7 seconds.

**Session termination:** The XR session transitions through `FOCUSED`, `VISIBLE`, `SYNCHRONIZED`, `STOPPING`, and finally `IDLE`. These transitions are consistent with the application session ending.

## 4. Key Behavioural Findings

### 4.1 Right-Hand Dominance

The Right controller shows more movement activity than the Left controller.

| Metric | Count |
|---|---:|
| Right Controller Moving | 1,692 rows |
| Left Controller Moving | 1,111 rows |
| Both Controllers Moving | 954 rows |

| Neither Controller Moving | 361 rows |

These results suggest greater reliance on the Right controller, particularly during the first 50 seconds of the session.

### 4.2 Whole-Body or Player-Rig Movement

Between 60 and 100 seconds, both controllers exhibit nearly identical movement speeds.

Examples include:

- **At approximately 70 s:** Left = 1.73 m/s, Right = 1.70 m/s.
- **At approximately 90 s:** Left = 2.08 m/s, Right = 2.10 m/s.

The midpoint between the two controllers travelled approximately 63.8 m, while the relative movement between the controllers covered approximately 18.4 m.

This pattern suggests that a substantial portion of the observed movement may result from movement of the player rig through the virtual environment, rather than independent hand movements alone.

Possible explanations include joystick-based locomotion or other in-game movement mechanics. The exact mechanism cannot be confirmed from controller pose data alone.

### 4.3 Vertical Position Transition

Two major vertical transitions were observed:

- **Upward transition:** Around 58.9 seconds, both controllers rise by approximately 3.3 m.
- **Downward transition:** Around 101.4–101.6 seconds, both controllers return to a lower vertical position.

Because the changes occur simultaneously in both controllers, they may reflect a shared change in the virtual player's position, such as an in-game lift, teleportation, or movement between platforms.

Speed spikes of up to approximately 110 were recorded around these transitions. These values may result from large positional changes over short time intervals and should be investigated before being interpreted as actual physical movement speeds.

The precise cause of the transitions remains unconfirmed.

### 4.4 Fine and Slow Movements

Most recorded movements are relatively slow, with occasional faster bursts.

|---|---:|---:|
| Median Speed for Moving Rows | 0.10 m/s | 0.16 m/s |
| 95th Percentile Speed | Approximately 3 m/s | Approximately 3 m/s |

The low median speeds suggest that many movements were small and controlled, while the higher percentile values indicate occasional periods of faster movement.

These measurements describe logged controller movement and do not independently establish the user's physical movement speed.

### 4.5 Peak Activity Window

The most active period occurred between approximately 70 and 100 seconds.

During this interval, both controllers were moving in roughly 82–98% of samples.

This period is characterised by sustained movement and highly similar controller speeds, consistent with coordinated movement through the virtual environment.

## 5. Limitations

Several limitations should be considered when interpreting these findings:

- **No head-pose data:** Headset position and orientation were not recorded, preventing analysis of gaze direction and head movement.
- **No hand-tracking data:** The dataset contains controller poses rather than detailed hand or finger movements.
- **No button or trigger events:** Actions such as grabbing, clicking, or activating objects cannot be confirmed.
- **Limited interaction context:** Only movement events are available, so actual object interactions cannot be distinguished reliably from general controller movement.
- **Unverified speed definition:** The logged speed is approximately 0.9 times the speed calculated from position and time. The logger implementation should be checked to establish the exact calculation.
- **Unconfirmed vertical transitions:** The events around 58.9 and 101.4 seconds require validation against the VR scene and application mechanics.
- **Potential movement artefacts:** Large position changes may produce unusually high calculated speeds and should be handled carefully during preprocessing.
- **Limited session duration:** The approximately 111-second recording may not capture enough behavioural variation to establish stable, long-term user movement patterns.

## 6. Conclusion

The VR controller log reveals distinct movement patterns throughout the recorded session.

The user initially relies more heavily on the Right controller, followed by a period of increased activity involving both controllers. Between approximately 60 and 100 seconds, the controllers exhibit closely matched speeds and remain at an elevated vertical position. Both controllers subsequently return to a lower position, and movement activity decreases before the session ends.

The findings suggest that the session includes both individual controller movements and substantial shared movement within the virtual environment. However, the available data does not establish the exact in-game actions or the mechanism responsible for the observed position changes.

## 7. Recommended Improvements

Future versions of the VR interaction logger should capture additional information to support more reliable behavioural analysis.

Recommended additions include:

1. **Headset Pose Logging:** Record HMD position and orientation to analyse head movement alongside controller behaviour.
2. **Button and Trigger Events:** Log button presses, trigger activation, grip inputs, and other controller actions.
3. **Scene and Object Identifiers:** Record the active scene and interacted object to distinguish intentional interactions from general movement.
4. **Locomotion Event Logging:** Record joystick input, teleportation events, player-rig transformations, and other locomotion-related changes.
5. **Improved Speed Validation:** Verify the speed calculation in the logger code and identify potential artefacts caused by sudden position changes.
6. **Temporal Feature Extraction:** Calculate movement statistics over fixed time windows to capture changes in behaviour throughout the session.
7. **Data Quality Checks:** Detect missing samples, timestamp inconsistencies, and abrupt positional discontinuities before analysis.

Combining controller poses with head movement, input events, and scene information would provide a stronger foundation for distinguishing controller interaction from locomotion and for conducting future VR behavioural analysis.
